# Preamble

The Preamble is the introductory statement of the Constitution — its "identity card", in the words of N. A. Palkhivala. It is based on the **Objectives Resolution**, drafted and moved by **Jawaharlal Nehru on 13 December 1946** and adopted by the Constituent Assembly on **22 January 1947**. K. M. Munshi called it the *horoscope of our sovereign democratic republic*; Sir Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar said it carried the essence of the entire Constitution.

:::predict Before you read on — which single amendment is the only one ever made to the Preamble?
The **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**. It inserted **socialist**, **secular** and **integrity** — and nothing else has been changed since.
:::

## The four ingredients

Read the Preamble as four parts; almost every question attacks one of them.

1. **Source of authority** — the Constitution derives its authority from *the people of India*, not from a Crown, a Parliament or a founding document.
2. **Nature of the state** — a **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic**.
3. **Objectives** — **Justice** (social, economic, political), **Liberty** (of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship), **Equality** (of status and of opportunity) and **Fraternity** (assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation).
4. **Date of adoption** — **26 November 1949**.

> [!note] The words **socialist**, **secular** and **integrity** were added by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** — the only amendment to the Preamble so far.

Watch the two dates. **26 November 1949** is the date the Constitution was *adopted*; it *commenced* on **26 January 1950**. The Preamble names only the first.

## The key words, decoded

- **Sovereign** — India is subject to no external authority. It may acquire or cede territory, and its membership of the Commonwealth or the United Nations does not dilute this.
- **Socialist** — **democratic socialism**, i.e. a mixed economy where public and private sectors coexist, *not* communistic socialism (state ownership of all means of production).
- **Secular** — the **positive** Indian model: the state has no religion of its own and gives every faith equal protection (Articles 25–28), rather than the Western wall-of-separation model.
- **Democratic** — political, social and economic democracy, worked through **representative parliamentary democracy**: universal adult franchise, periodic elections, rule of law, an independent judiciary, no discrimination.
- **Republic** — the head of state is **elected, not hereditary**; every public office is open to every citizen without privilege.

## Where the ideals came from

- **Liberty, Equality, Fraternity** — the **French Revolution (1789)**.
- **Justice** — social, economic and political — the **Russian Revolution (1917)**.
- The **Republic** and the elected head of state — the French and American models.

## Is the Preamble part of the Constitution?

- **Berubari Union case (1960):** the Supreme Court held the Preamble is *not* a part of the Constitution, though it is a key to the minds of the makers.
- **Kesavananda Bharati case (1973):** overruled Berubari — the Preamble **is** a part of the Constitution and can be amended under **Article 368**, subject to the **basic structure** doctrine.
- **LIC of India case (1995):** reaffirmed that the Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution.

Two limits follow, and both are examined constantly. The Preamble is **non-justiciable** — its provisions are not enforceable in a court. And it is **neither a source of power nor a limitation on power** for the legislature or the executive. What it does do is aid the interpretation of ambiguous constitutional provisions and help identify the basic structure.

:::recall Explain in one line why the Preamble matters in court even though it is non-justiciable.
It is not directly enforceable and grants no power, but courts use it to **interpret ambiguous provisions** and to identify the **basic structure** — so it shapes outcomes without conferring rights on its own.
:::
