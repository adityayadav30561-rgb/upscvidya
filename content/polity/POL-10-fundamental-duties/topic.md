# Fundamental Duties

Fundamental Duties are contained in **Part IVA**, which consists of a single article — **Article 51A**. They were added by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee**, and are inspired by the Constitution of the **erstwhile USSR**.

## Count and evolution

- The Swaran Singh Committee recommended **8** duties; the 42nd Amendment enacted **10**.
- The **86th Amendment Act, 2002** added the **11th duty** — Article 51A(k): a parent or guardian shall provide opportunities for education to their child or ward between the ages of **6 and 14 years**.

## Key features

- Apply **only to citizens**, not to foreigners (unlike some Fundamental Rights that extend to all persons).
- **Non-justiciable**: not enforceable by courts; no penalty is prescribed by Article 51A itself. Parliament may enforce specific duties through ordinary law (e.g., Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971).
- They serve as an interpretive aid — courts may use them to test the reasonableness of laws.

## Notable duties (Article 51A)

- (a) abide by the Constitution, respect the National Flag and National Anthem
- (g) protect and improve the **natural environment** including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife, and have compassion for living creatures
- (h) develop scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform
- (j) strive towards excellence in all spheres
- (k) provide education opportunities to children aged 6-14 (added 2002)
