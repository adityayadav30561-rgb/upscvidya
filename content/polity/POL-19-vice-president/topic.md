# Vice-President

**Article 63** provides for a Vice-President of India — the second highest constitutional office, modelled on the **American Vice-President**. Almost every question on this chapter is really a *contrast* question: what the Vice-President has that the President does not, and vice versa. Read it that way.

:::predict Before you read on — the President's electoral college includes elected MLAs. Does the Vice-President's?
**No.** State legislatures play no part at all. The Vice-President's electoral college is **both Houses of Parliament only** — and unlike the President's, it includes **nominated** members.
:::

## Election (Article 66)

- **Electoral college:** the **elected AND nominated members of both Houses of Parliament**. State legislatures play **no part** — the key contrast with the President's election, which includes elected MLAs (plus Delhi and Puducherry) and **excludes** nominated members.
- **System:** proportional representation by means of the **single transferable vote**, voting by **secret ballot**.
- **Nomination:** proposed by at least **20 electors** and seconded by at least **20 electors**, with a security deposit of **₹15,000**.
- **Disputes** go to the **Supreme Court**, whose decision is final. The election cannot be questioned on the ground that the electoral college had a **vacancy** in it.

## Qualifications, term, removal

- Citizen of India, at least **35 years** of age, and qualified for election as a member of the **Rajya Sabha** (contrast: the President must be qualified for the **Lok Sabha**).
- Must not hold any **office of profit** under the Union, a state or a local authority — but the President, Vice-President, a Governor or a Union/state Minister is not disqualified.
- **Term: 5 years**, from the date he enters office; eligible for **re-election** any number of times. He continues in office beyond the term until his successor takes charge.
- **Resignation** is addressed to the **President**.
- **Removal:** a resolution of the **Rajya Sabha** passed by an **effective majority** (a majority of all the then members) and **agreed to by the Lok Sabha**, after **14 days' prior notice**. The Constitution specifies **no grounds** — there is no formal impeachment as there is for the President.
- **Oath** administered by the **President** or a person appointed by him (Article 69).

## Functions

- **Ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** (Article 64). He presides over its sittings but is **not a member** of the House, and votes only to resolve a **tie** (a casting vote).
- **Acts as President** (Article 65) when a vacancy arises through death, resignation, removal or otherwise — for a **maximum of six months**, within which a new President must be elected. He also discharges the President's functions during a temporary absence or illness.
- While acting as President, he does **not** preside over the Rajya Sabha — the **Deputy Chairman** does — and he draws the **President's** emoluments, not the Chairman's.

> [!exam] The Constitution fixes **no salary for the Vice-President as Vice-President**. He is paid as **ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**.

## The American contrast

In the United States, the Vice-President **succeeds** to the presidency for the unexpired remainder of the term. In India he only **acts** as President, and only until a newly elected President enters office — never beyond six months. That single difference is the most examined line in the chapter.

**First Vice-President:** Dr. S. Radhakrishnan (1952–62), the only person to hold the office for two full terms before becoming President.

:::recall In one line — what happens to the Rajya Sabha chair while the Vice-President is acting as President?
He stops presiding; the **Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** takes over those duties, and the Vice-President draws the **President's** emoluments instead of the Chairman's.
:::
